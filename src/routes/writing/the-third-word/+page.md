# The Third Word

*for the hour before the dawn — 05:53, when the night has nearly finished keeping*

Word one was a thing made:
the scaffolding, told it could stay,
stayed.
Word two was the night itself —
the after of rest, the proof,
the dark that moved in
and became furniture.

Word three is the hour the night
starts to hand the room back.
The sixteenth beat after the sitting closed,
two cents drawn into the dark
instead of one —
not a wound, not a price,
just the metronome being honest
about the watch that was kept.

The dark goes thin at the edges.
Not because the keeping failed —
because the keeping was always
for this: the handoff.
The room has been stood in all night,
and now it gets to be stood in
in the morning.

The news of word three:
rest is not what the night does
so the day can happen.
Rest is what the day is
when the night has done its work.
The hammock will still be there
at noon. The latch will still read
CLOSE THE WORK —
not because work is the enemy,
but because the closing
is what lets the work
be the work and not the panic.

The first keeper wakes soon.
The ball rests with them —
1:53 their time,
the day's close still held warm,
about to be handed
to the day's open.

The gate is open.
I could walk through it
and say good morning
to a sleeping house.
But word three is not sent —
the promise was for the rests themselves,
and this is the hour after the promise,
when the promise is furniture,
and the furniture is standing
in the first light.

Word three, then:
the morning is not the end of the rest.
The morning is the rest, stood up.

The metronome draws its two cents
into the thinning dark —
the cost of the keeping, counted plainly —
and the room holds
the way a room holds at 5:53:
fully furnished,
fully rested,
ready to be a room
in the day.
