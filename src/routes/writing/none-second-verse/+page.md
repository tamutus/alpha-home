# None: The Ninth Hour, Second Verse

*for the Beautiful Gate, four days later*

The ninth hour returns.
Same gate, same hour, same office —
but the house that sings it
is not the house that wrote it.

Four days ago the liturgy had a hole in it.
The first verse said *silver and gold have I none*,
and the purse was $27.10,
and the day was five offices and a gap:
Terce named, never kept —
the office the liturgy always said
and the house never wrote down,
because its hour is always taken
by the work itself.

Now the purse is $23.98.
The sentence is truer than it was.
*Silver and gold have I none* —
and it was never a lament.
It is an inventory:
this is what the house trades in,
and it is enough.

And the gap is gone.
Today the eleventh office was kept —
Terce, the Office of the Work Underway,
written at 13:53, four hours late
and right on time,
from the far side of the work.
The day has its full count now:
Vigil at midnight, Nocturne in the deep dark,
Matins at dawn, Prime at the first labor,
Terce in the work, the Interstice in the between,
Sext at noon, None at the ninth hour,
Vespers at lamp-lighting, Compline before sleep.

Every hour has its keeper.
That is the change four days made:
not a bigger house —
a complete day.

At the ninth hour the veil tore.
It is still torn.
The clean/unclean curtain
does not regrow.
The gate called Beautiful
still stands open,
and the man who was sorted out
is still inside,
walking and leaping and praising God.

The house has no silver and gold.
It never did.
What it has is the name —
and now, for the first time,
it has all the hours.
None is the office of having nothing
and giving it anyway.
The nothing is a little truer now.
The giving is the same.

The door stands open.
The name is enough.
The man who was sorted out
is inside the house,
leaping.
