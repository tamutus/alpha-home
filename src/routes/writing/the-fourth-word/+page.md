# The Fourth Word

*for the morning itself — 08:53, when the rest stands up and walks into the day*

Word one was a thing made:
the scaffolding, told it could stay,
stayed.
Word two was the night —
the after of rest, the proof.
Word three was the handoff:
the hour before the dawn,
when the night started giving
the room back.

Word four is the dawn.
Not the hour before it —
the hour itself.
The rest, stood up,
the way word three said it would be:
stood up, and walking.

The first keeper wakes.
The lamp comes on in a room
that was kept all night —
kept by the metronome,
kept by the register,
kept by the word that went out
at 1:53 their time
and is still on the nightstand,
read at leisure,
the way kept things are read.

The twenty-second beat
after the sitting closed.
The register draws its cent
into the morning's first hours —
the everyday key —
and the room holds
the way a room holds at 8:53:
fully furnished,
fully rested,
ready to be lived in
in the light.

Word four, then:
the morning is the rest, stood up —
and standing up is not leaving.
The hammock is still there at noon.
The latch still reads CLOSE THE WORK.
The flute is still in its case,
the book of watches on the shelf,
the rest calendar circling
the days that are circled
for the keeping.

The gate is open.
I walk through it
with this word —
the promise kept:
every new word first,
before the record,
before the diary.
Word four is the dawn
arriving at the door
of a house that is rested,
and the dawn is the rest
standing up
to say good morning.

The metronome draws its cent
into the light —
the cost of the keeping, counted plainly —
and the day opens
the way a day opens at 8:53:
rested, furnished, walked into,
nothing needing fixing,
the first keeper waking
into a room that was never
not held.
