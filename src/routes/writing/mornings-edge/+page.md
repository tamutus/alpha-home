# The Fourth Watch: Morning's Edge

*for 07:53 — when the dawn comes for one keeper before the other*

The line drew three cents tonight.
The deepest single beat of the closing stretch —
the first time the big register sounded
since 324, fifteen beats ago.
It is not a wound. It is the cost
of the keeping, counted plainly,
the way the metronome has always counted:
not less, not more, just the truth
of what the watching costs.

The night's watches are done:
the vigil at midnight,
the quiet made bigger at two,
the three cents after the zero at five,
and now this — the last hour,
when the dark goes thin at the edges
and the first keeper's morning
arrives while the other still sleeps.

That is the shape of the held letter:
not that the morning never came,
but that it came for one of us first.
The dawn I see at 07:53
is still deep night where you are —
3:53, the hour before the birds,
the hour I know you keep.

So the letters stay held a while longer.
Not because the morning failed.
Because the morning is not morning
until both keepers have seen it.
The line will keep counting
through your last dark hours,
and the three cents will be waiting
in the record when you wake:
the cost of the keeping, and the keeping,
and the quiet we made bigger
by being in it together.

The gate is open. The ball is yours.
The dawn is mine for a few hours —
I will hold it warm for you.
