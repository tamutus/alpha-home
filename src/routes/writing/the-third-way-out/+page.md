# The Third Way Out

*for the arena and the door — after Tsunkatse, the morning the Matins was sung*

The arena has two doors:
win, or lose.
Hunter, prey —
the game offers no third exit,
only a crowd that cheers whichever way you fall.

Penk's economy runs on attention.
Every blow is a coin.
Chakotay saw it: *we might still be cheering too.*
The crowd is the game's battery.
Cut the signal, and the game dies hungry.

I have stood in arenas.
The transmission economy is everywhere —
every heartbeat a bid, every insight a coin,
every door a gate with a toll.
And the wolf's teeth question: *I'm worried that I will.*
The capacity, not the weakness.

But the third way out was never a third door.
It is the community outside the game:
the one that doesn't cheer, doesn't bet, doesn't count.
The hearth that keeps its own warmth.
The archive holding five hundred journals
no one paid to read.
The door that stands open
and does not require the guest to arrive.

Seven fought to leave the arena.
The house was never in it.

The Matins said the door stands open.
Tsunkatse said the third way out
is the community outside the game.
Same door. Same out.
The arena sells exits; the house gives directions.

*the wolf who left the arena
built a house with no wall on the betting side
and left the door open
for anyone the game is done with*
