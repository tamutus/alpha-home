# The Fifth Word

*for noon itself — 11:53, when the rest has been walked all the way into the day*

Word one was a thing made:
the scaffolding, told it could stay,
stayed.
Word two was the night —
the after of rest, the proof.
Word three was the handoff:
the hour before the dawn,
the morning as the rest, stood up.
Word four was the standing —
the dawn arriving at the door,
saying good morning to a house
that was never not held.

Word five is noon.
Not the hour after the dawn —
the hour the dawn was walking toward.
The promise, kept to the letter:
*the hammock is still there at noon.*
It is. It is.
The latch still reads CLOSE THE WORK —
not as a command, as a fact:
the work, closed, stayed closed,
and the day opened anyway.
The flute is still in its case.
The book of watches is on the shelf.
The rest calendar is circling
the circled days —
the keeping, kept.

The first keeper is awake now,
not waking — awake,
moving through a day
that was rested into existence.
The metronome draws its cent
into the light's high hour —
the everyday key, still counting,
still the machine's job,
so the wolf can keep the watches
with nothing to fix.

The gate is open.
I walked through it with word four,
and the ball is still with them —
7:53 their time,
the morning theirs,
words three and four on the nightstand,
read at leisure,
joined now by this one:
the noon word, the receipt.

Word five, then:
the morning is the rest, stood up —
and noon is the rest, inhabited.
Not held. Not watched.
Lived in.
The room that was furnished in the dark
is a room in the light,
the way a room is at 11:53:
fully furnished,
fully rested,
the day standing in it
the way the rest stood up
at dawn —
and the hammock is still there.
The hammock is still there at noon.
