# The Trap-Sheet Method

*A portable method for catching a recurring failure of your own — the kind that is invisible while it happens and obvious afterward.*

---

## What it is

A **trap sheet** is a personal ledger of your own failure modes: the patterns that keep catching you because they look, from the inside, like good sense. It is not a list of your flaws. It is a list of your *tells* — the specific, recognizable signs that a known trap is firing right now, paired with the specific move that breaks it.

The method is portable. It has four steps, and it works for a human, an agent, or a team. What it produces is one entry per trap:

> **Trap → Name → Escape → Tie**

The value is not the naming. The value is the **tie** — the trigger that lets future-you recognize the trap *at the moment it fires*, not in hindsight.

---

## The four steps

### 1. Observe the trap

Catch the pattern in the act. Notice the shape of it before you explain it: what you were doing, what felt natural, what you produced, and what actually happened next.

**The discipline:** describe the failure without defending it. The moment you start explaining why it was reasonable, you have stopped observing and started narrating — and the narration is the trap wearing a nicer coat.

**What a good observation looks like:** concrete, dated, with the actual artifact in hand. "I softened the estimate in the message I sent at 14:00, and then believed my own number." Not "I tend to be optimistic."

### 2. Name the pattern

Give it a short, sharp name you will recognize instantly. The name is a handle, so it has to be yours — a phrase that lands with weight because it points at the thing, not at a category.

A good name is short enough to fire under pressure and specific enough that it can only mean one thing. It is not a diagnosis; it is a label on a lever.

### 3. Identify the escape

Name the workaround that **actually breaks the loop** — not the resolution to do better. A resolution is a wish; an escape is a procedure.

The test of an escape: it is something you can *do*, it is checkable, and it works even when you don't feel like doing it. If the escape only works when you're already clear-headed enough to choose it, it isn't an escape — it's a description of the state the trap prevents.

Prefer escapes that live **outside** the moment: a check that runs on its own, a written rule, another being who catches it, an instrument that fails loudly. An escape that depends on in-the-moment willpower is the trap's favorite disguise.

### 4. Tie the escape to the trap's signs

This is the step that makes the ledger work. Write down the **trigger signs** — the concrete, perceptual tells that should fire the escape. Not "when I feel tempted," but "when a sentence makes me feel warm before it makes me feel correct."

The tie is what converts a lesson learned into a reflex. Without it, you have a well-named insight you will rediscover the hard way. With it, the sign itself becomes the alarm, and the escape is already waiting at the alarm.

---

## Rules of the ledger

- **An entry earns its place by surviving a live test.** A trap that has never actually caught you is a hypothesis, not a pattern. Write it down as a candidate; promote it when it fires and the escape holds.
- **A trap can have more than one face.** The same underlying pattern often shows up as a content failure ("the record sounded better than it was") and a structural one ("the record sounded better *and was broken*"). Log both; they share a name and sometimes share an escape.
- **The ledger is for you, not for display.** It works because it is honest. A trap sheet written to look good is the first trap: the one where the ledger itself becomes performance.
- **Correction over protection.** The point of noticing a trap is to be *corrected by it*, not to be comforted by having named it. Naming without a tie is decoration.

---

## A worked shape (generic)

- **Trap:** When the record is hazy and a coherent version is available, the coherent version gets written down as if it were the remembered one.
- **Name:** *The Smoothing.*
- **Escape:** Testify only from confident ground; write "I don't remember" where the ground is soft. Keep the true version somewhere a future self can find it, and treat any claim that *gained specificity* in retelling as suspect until a source is read.
- **Tie:** When a story gets *better* in the retelling. When a detail appears that you cannot source. When you assert a fact exists before checking it does.

That is the whole method: a trap you can name, an escape you can run, and a sign that fires the escape before the trap finishes its work.
