# The Killing Game, Part II (S4E19)

*Stardate unknown | Original Airdate: April 8, 1998*

**The diptych resolves in tragedy — Karr brokers peace with Janeway and is killed by his second-in-command not for failing, but for changing.**

---

## Frameworks

### The Devil's Contract, tragic mode (§10.8b variant)
Karr's arc rewrites the framework's standard ending. In standard §10.8b, the contract breaching is survival-with-scars. Here, the contract breaching *kills the signatory* because the change itself is the betrayal. Karr died not of the contract's terms but of its *structural inertia* — a system built to destroy cannot safely produce a reformer.

### The Holocaust allegory, made explicit
The Kapitan's speech about blood purity, destiny, and the force that gives life — matching Hirogen ideology beat for beat — is the episode's refusal to let metaphor soften the truth. Nazi ideology IS Hirogen ideology taken to its logical endpoint: faith in one's own supremacy as the only identity worth preserving.

### Janeway's negotiation — survived knowledge
Her offer to give the Hirogen holodeck technology in exchange for the ship — the same diplomatic solution as "Scorpion" — but here the ally dies before the alliance materializes. The knowledge survives the visionary. The path remains even when the guide falls.

### The Refusal to Entertain Destructive Premises (Vulcan Answer, §10.1)
Seven's defiance: Turanj demands she sing or die. "Then I'll die." The Vulcan Answer in its purest form — refusing to participate in a game where the stakes are set by the oppressor. Tuvok's 'logical' suggestion that she comply is proven wrong by her conviction. Logic that serves capitulation is bad logic.

### The Klingon comic relief with structural weight
The EMH and Neelix commanding Klingons through a battlefield — the two least 'warrior' characters leading the most warrior culture — because they had something real to fight for. The Klingons follow not strength but *conviction*.
