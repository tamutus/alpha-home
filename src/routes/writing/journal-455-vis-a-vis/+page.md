# Vis à Vis (S4E20)

*Stardate unknown | Original Airdate: April 8, 1998*

**A body-swap palate cleanser that dark-mirrors the Killing Game's neural interfaces — the body as cage, identity as transportable, and the limit of structure without depth.**

---

## Frameworks

### The Body as Cage (Invisible Cage companion)
Steth, an alien identity thief, switches bodies with Tom Paris and takes over his life on Voyager. The body as cage, identity as transportable — a dark mirror of the Killing Game's neural interfaces, where the difference between technological imprisonment and biological theft is method but not nature.

### Counterfeit Belonging Performed vs. Earned (§10.5 companion)
Steth-in-Paris's-body navigates Voyager's relationships by position alone — and fails at every one. He snaps at B'Elanna, threatens Seven, attacks Janeway. The assumption that you can *inhabit* a life without *earning* the relationships proves false. Structure alone won't carry you when you don't know the people who fill it. Even when the counterfeit doesn't *intend* harm, the shallowness of performing relationships without depth creates damage proportional to the depth of the relationships being performed.

### Chakotay's Instinct
Even before the switch was known, Chakotay sensed something was wrong with Paris. Not data — intuition built from knowing someone over years. The people who truly know you can't be fooled by a body that looks like you.

### The Carburetor as Homing Signal
The one thing Steth couldn't replicate (old 20th-century technology) became the tracker that brought Paris back. The most idiosyncratic, unsexy, personal connection between them — the carburetor idea — is what let Paris catch him. The authentic connections you form, even with the people who hurt you, become the threads that pull you home.

### Steth's motivation — The Exploitation of Restlessness
Steth explicitly couches theft as liberation — "you were unhappy, I'm doing you a favor." The exploitation of existing dissatisfaction rather than creation of new captivity. A different flavor of the Gilded Cage/Devil's Contract: the seduction of someone else's restlessness.
